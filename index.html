Plan the request

Sketching which rooms and floors a problem affects

A symptom that appears in more than one place may come from a single source or from several, and the pattern is easy to lose in a description. If a stain, smell, noise or weak flow shows up on different floors of your Thornhill home, draw a rough sketch of each floor and mark every fixture, noting which ones show the symptom and which do not. A provider can then ask better questions before anything is planned.

  • Mark every fixture

    Draw each floor roughly and mark every sink, toilet, shower, tub and appliance, including ones that seem unaffected.

  • Show where it appears

    Circle the places where the symptom shows, and note the room directly above and below each one.

  • Equipment on the sketch

    Add the water heater, sump pit, softener or filter and main shutoff, so the sketch shows how they sit relative to the symptom.

Before you send

What to Photograph Before Requesting a Quote

A few clear photos help a provider understand the job before the first call. Have them ready for the follow-up.

  • Water heater: the label with the model and capacity, the vent connection at the top of the tank, and any rental sticker or agreement.
  • Blocked drain: the fixtures that drain slowly or back up, whether it's only the lowest level, and the floor drain or cleanout if it's accessible.
  • Leak: the wet area or stain, the water meter with all fixtures turned off, and any recent work near the leak.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the line beside the water meter. Copper is orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey with threaded joints, and lead is dull grey and shows bright silver when scratched lightly with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Thornhill Request

Should I send one request if a symptom shows in several Thornhill rooms?

Yes, one request is usually clearer when the symptoms may be connected, such as a stain below a bathroom and a slow drain on the same floor. List each room separately within it and mark them on one sketch. Unrelated items, like a worn tap and a softener question, are better sent as separate requests.

How detailed does the sketch of my Thornhill home need to be?

A rough hand sketch is enough. Draw each floor as a box, mark every sink, toilet, shower and appliance, circle the ones that show the symptom, and label the water heater, pit and treatment equipment if you have them. It records what you observe; it is not a plan, a measurement or a diagnosis.

Once my Thornhill request and sketch are sent, is a visit already set up?

No. A request only reaches our enquiry team for review; it does not establish that anyone covers your address or has agreed a price. A provider must still confirm the address, the work, their qualifications, the written scope, pricing and scheduling, and until then nothing is booked and you have committed to nothing.

How do I show a stain upstairs and a slow drain downstairs?

Draw both floors side by side, with the stain circled on one and the slow fixture circled on the other, and align the rooms so that anything directly above or below another is clear. State which problem you noticed first. The respondent can then judge whether one cause or two should be explored, without you needing to trace anything behind walls.

Should I include the water heater and sump pit in a Thornhill sketch?

Yes, if you have them, along with any softener, filter and main shutoff. Equipment placement helps show whether a symptom follows the hot water, the drains, the pit or the treated lines. Mark them even when you think they are unrelated, because the respondent may see a pattern you have not noticed.

Who decides whether a sketch is enough to plan the work?

The provider decides what they need to see and may ask for more photographs, labels or access before proposing anything. The sketch is a way to organize what you have observed, not a substitute for their assessment. Ask what they still need, and confirm who can authorize work and who can open the door.
